The Parseh guide

Parseh is a Frank-method toolbox: books to read with the translation woven in, videos to watch with a transcript that follows the voice, a studio to write your own lessons, and exercise decks to study — in eleven languages, from Persian and Arabic, right to left, to Japanese and Chinese, set vertically when you want them so.

Welcome

Parseh runs on your own computer, and you use it in a browser — on that computer, or on a phone that can reach it. It needs no account, and what you read and write in it stays in its folder. This guide explains every part of it, one section a part, with pictures and with examples that work on the page.

If you are new, begin with Getting started: it takes you from installing Parseh, on Linux, macOS or Windows, to its first page, the hub, and from there to a phone.

  • Reference and troubleshootingWhat a message means, every command for those who want one, where each file lives, and the words Parseh uses.
  • Writing this guideHow these pages are written — Hugo’s Markdown and the studio’s dialect, code blocks, pictures, shortcodes — compiled and published.

Finding your way

Every page is in the list on the left — the ☰ button opens it on a phone — and the search box above it finds any word on any page; press / to jump into it. At the foot of each page, Previous and Next walk the whole guide in order; the ◐ button changes the theme, light, dark or sepia.
