A PDF reaches a reader on paper. **Download ▾ → HTML page, for a website
(.html)** reaches one on a screen who has no Parseh: a student who opens a
link on your class's website. It is **one file**. Put it on a website, send
it, or open it straight from the disk: it works with no server of any kind
behind it. Its exercises can be answered and checked, its recordings play,
its pictures are there. Everything it shows travels inside the file.

## Making one

On the document's page, open **Download ▾** and choose **HTML page, for a
website (.html)**, right under **PDF**. The file comes after a moment,
named after the document: `verbs-of-motion.html` for
`/studio/doc/verbs-of-motion-3f9a1c`. Nothing has to be built first, and
the document itself is not touched.

The page is made from the document **as it is saved**: save in the editor
first.

A deck's exercises can be a page too, one that crams them: see
[Cram mode](../exercises/cram.md#as-a-page-for-a-website).

## What the page has

- **The document as it reads.** The same sheet as its page in the studio,
  with its colours, numbered sections, lemma headings, boxes, tables and
  formulas. A footnote's number shows the note in a cloud when you hover
  over it or tap it.
- **Its exercises, working.** Every exercise can be answered, and
  **Check exercises** at the end marks them all and gives the score, as on
  the studio's page. Flashcards turn when you click them, their 🔊 plays,
  **⤢ Enlarge** opens one large, and **Hide transliterations** works.
- **Its pictures and recordings, inside the file.** Where a recording is
  played only in part (`start=0 end=0.4`), that stretch is cut out when
  the file is made, as long as ffmpeg is installed on your computer
  ([Installing](../getting-started/installing.md)). A page that plays one
  word of a long recording then carries only that word. Without ffmpeg the
  whole recording goes in, once, and the page plays its stretch of it.
- **A video, in YouTube's own player.** It is the one thing the page
  reaches out for. It needs the internet, and it plays only when the page
  is on a website: opened from the disk, YouTube refuses it with *Video
  player configuration error*. A player has to tell YouTube which site it
  is on, and a file on the disk is on no site. So there, and only there, a
  line under the video says so: *YouTube plays this video only when the
  page is on a website — watch it on YouTube*, the last words opening the
  video on YouTube, from where the page's stretch of it begins.
- **Contents.** At the top, when the document has sections: a list that
  opens and shuts, whose entries jump to their section.
- **Glosses.** Under the contents: the document's glosses as a table that
  opens and shuts, with a box that filters them as you type. The studio's
  flashcard drill of the glosses is not on the page.
- **Aa.** At the top right: the size of the text and the theme, **Paper**,
  **Sepia** or **Dark**. The page starts on **Sepia**, the warm paper made
  for reading, whatever the studio was showing when you made it and
  whatever the student's computer or phone prefers; a student who likes
  another picks it here, until the tab is closed.
- **Its foot.** *This page was exported from Parseh*, with links to
  **Parseh on GitHub** and to **Parseh's guide**, this one. Each opens in a
  new tab, so the page, and every answer given on it, stays as it was.

## What it leaves out

The page is **read-only by construction**. A student can answer it, never
change it.

- **No Markdown.** What goes in is the typeset page, never its source: not
  the Markdown of a target-language run, not a line number, nothing the
  editor finds a line by. The document's Markdown can be neither read nor
  pieced back together from the file.
- **Nothing that edits.** There is no ✎, no ⚙ on a picture or a player,
  no **+ Deck** and no colour palette, and none of the code behind them is
  in the file.
- **No links to your other documents.** A link to another document of the
  library (`[…](doc:Name)`) keeps its words and loses the link, and the
  other document's name goes with it: that document is not on the website.
  Links to web pages stay links.
- **No technical note, no print button.** The *Nota tecnica* at the end
  of the studio's page is left off. The browser's own **Print** prints the
  page without its bar, its panels and the Aa menu, and the links of its
  foot print with their addresses.

## It keeps nothing

The page keeps nothing and sends nothing. The size and theme chosen with
**Aa**, the answers given, the transliterations hidden: all of it lasts
until the tab is closed or reloaded, and none of it is written anywhere.
Nothing goes into the browser's storage or a cookie, and nothing to any
server. The page tells the browser so itself: its own security rules forbid
it to send anything, or to fetch anything but a YouTube player. So the
rules hold on a website you do not run, too. A link a student follows from
the page does not say where it was followed from, and the YouTube player
is told the site's address and nothing more.

The answers are in the file, as they are in the studio's page: a page that
marks answers has to know them. A student who reads the file's source can
find them. A page on a website is for practice, not for an exam.

## How big it is

About half a megabyte for a short note: the page's styles, the script its
exercises need, and the faces it is set in, cut down to the letters the
page uses. Its pictures and recordings add their own size. **A formula
anywhere adds about 2 MB**, because the first formula brings MathJax whole,
the typesetter that draws formulas. A page without formulas carries none of
it.
